Part I01 — The cash loop

Your money goes on a 110-day trip. Everything else is detail.

This is the entire business. Cash leaves you, becomes pens, crosses a border, sits on a shelf, and comes back bigger. How many times a year that loop closes is your return.

The two things beginners get wrong

  • They think margin is the lever. It isn't. Going from 44% to 55% margin raises your annual return by about a quarter. Going from 2 turns a year to 4 doubles it. Speed beats markup.
  • They spend the returning cash. The green arrow says reinvest. JetPens went from $5,000 to $64.4M a year by never taking outside money and never pulling profit out early — profitable in two years, no advertising at the start.

Can you buy in bulk? Yes — three different ways

  • Bulk from Japan, lawfully. Register on Super Delivery or NETSEA as a business. No minimum order quantity on most items; shipping discounts start at 5% above ¥50,000 and reach 25% above ¥500,000. Authenticity is guaranteed. The open question is whether they ship to Bangladesh at all — their documentation names North America and Europe, so confirm this by email before you plan around it.
  • Bulk from China, in your own name. Alibaba.com suppliers ship internationally and Trade Assurance protects the payment. You appear as the importer, you get the Bill of Entry, and your AIT and AT become creditable. More paperwork, more legitimacy.
  • Bulk from China, through an agent. Cheapest per kilo by a wide margin and the least work. Two real costs: authenticity is your problem, and the declared-value question in section 07 is your liability.

For the personal-baggage route people will suggest: an adult's duty-free allowance is 65 kg of personal baggage. A genuine sample box fits comfortably. A full inventory batch is not personal baggage and will be assessed as commercial — don't build a plan on it.

Authentication: the fifteen minutes that protect the brand

Run this on every sample, from every new supplier, every time. Zebra, Uni and Pilot all manufacture in China, Indonesia and Vietnam for export — so genuine non-Japanese-made stock exists alongside convincing fakes, and you cannot tell them apart by country alone.

01

Buy one known-genuine reference unit

Retail, through a Japan proxy. You cannot detect a fake without something real beside it. Build a locked reference box of one verified unit per hero SKU and keep it permanently.

02

Check the JAN barcode prefix

Japanese-registered products begin 45 or 49. Necessary but not sufficient — export stock may differ legitimately. A prefix that doesn't match your reference unit for the same model is the red flag, not a wrong prefix as such.

03

Inspect the print quality

Genuine barcodes and brand marks are crisp and GS1-compliant. Smudged, misaligned or slightly-wrong-font printing is the most reliable single tell on counterfeit stationery.

04

Feel the moulding

Barrel seam, clip spring tension, cap click, grip texture. Copies get the colour right and the tolerances wrong.

05

Write-test on the paper you'll sell

Twenty lines, then check skipping, ink flow, dry time and bleed-through, side by side with the reference. Film it — this becomes your "how to spot a fake" video and your best marketing asset.

06

Sanity-check the price

A unit price far below 55% of Japan retail isn't a good deal, it's a different product.

07

Pay a deposit, not the full amount

30–50% down, balance on your inspection. A supplier who won't accept that is telling you something.

The cash trap in corporate orders

Companies in Bangladesh routinely pay on 15 to 45 day terms. Winning a ৳500,000 order means buying ৳300,000 of stock and funding it for six weeks before a taka arrives. On ৳300,000 of total capital, that single order can bankrupt you while looking like your best month ever.

The rule: on any first order from a new company, ask for 50% in advance. Frame it as standard for custom-branded goods, which it is. If they refuse and the order is large, walk away — or cap it at what you could survive losing. Once a client has paid you twice, extend terms.

The price ladder — design the catalogue so people climb it

Ten rules for keeping it premium

1 · Never discount the product. Discount the basket.

Free delivery, a free refill, gift wrapping, an extra colour — never "30% off Mildliner". The moment you discount a Japanese brand, your price becomes negotiable forever, and Daraz wins every negotiation.

2 · Publish every price. Never "inbox for price".

Almost every Tier C competitor hides prices in DMs. Hiding price signals that it's negotiable and that you're a person, not a shop. JAPspired publishes a full list — it's the main reason they read as legitimate at eight SKUs.

3 · One backdrop, one crop, forever.

Visual consistency is the premium. Forty products photographed identically read as a designed catalogue; forty photographed variously read as a reseller. This costs ৳200 and is the single highest-return decision in section 13.

4 · Spend on the box.

A ৳40 box on a ৳2,290 kit is 1.7% of revenue and 100% of the unboxing. It's also the thing that gets photographed and posted, which makes it marketing rather than cost.

5 · Never sell a fake — not once, not unknowingly.

Run the authentication protocol in section 07 on every batch, and publish the standard. In a market where Daraz mixes lookalikes into genuine listings, being verifiably real is the whole position.

6 · Say no to the wrong order.

Politely declining a ৳150 single order, with a line explaining the ৳300 minimum, protects the position better than taking it. Premium brands are defined as much by what they refuse.

7 · Publish your response hours, then hold them.

A twelve-hour silent DM destroys premium as fast as a bad photograph. Pens Galaxy BD publishes 11am–8pm. Pick a window you can actually keep and say it on the page.

8 · Use real scarcity, honestly.

Japan-only editions and Hobonichi covers genuinely sell out. "Twelve landed, four left" is true, is the strongest premium signal available, and costs nothing. Never fake it — one invented shortage undoes rule 5.

9 · Don't follow Daraz down.

List narrowly at your price. When someone undercuts you with a lookalike, answer with a comparison video, not a price cut. You cannot win a price war against a counterfeit and you don't need to.

10 · Educate instead of selling.

Explaining why a pencil costs ৳850 — the rotating lead, the ৳400 refill, the ten years of use — is what makes ৳850 feel cheap. The content strategy and the premium strategy are the same strategy.

The one-sentence test

Before any decision — a discount, a supplier, a photograph, a reply — ask: would the shop I want to be in five years do this? The answer is nearly always obvious, and nearly always slightly more expensive today. That gap is what the premium is.

  3. Seed 10 kits to nano creators (1k–10k followers, one tight niche, 6.2% Instagram / 10.3% TikTok engagement against 1–2% for large accounts). In Bangladesh they take product, not cash. No script, no approval — seeded content that looks like an ad performs like one.
  4. Campus consignment. One stall near a medical college or coaching centre. Someone else's footfall, no rent, no ad spend.
  5. Only then retarget — people who watched a comparison video and didn't buy. Never prospect cold until the four above are running.
  6. List narrowly on Rokomari and Daraz for discovery, not margin. Roughly 14% of order value goes to the platform, so put your defensible-price items there and keep the kits on your own site.

The rule that keeps this cheap

Cap paid advertising at 15% of revenue, and never spend a taka on cold prospecting until the free channels are running. Drag the ad slider in section 14 past 20% and watch the annual return fall — at 44% gross margin there simply isn't room for expensive traffic. JetPens reached profitability in two years having spent nothing on advertising at all, because they had "more time than money." So do you.

Part V16 — What kills you

Eight ways this goes wrong, with the numbers

Decide your response to each now, while it's cheap. Every one of these has killed a Bangladeshi f-commerce business.

1 · The batch is counterfeit

up to −৳195,000

You find out from a customer, publicly. In a category whose entire premium is authenticity, this costs the brand, not just the batch.

ResponsePaid samples before every bulk order, every supplier, every time. The reference-unit box in section 07. Deposit not full payment. Keep a small genuinely Japan-sourced range as your published standard.

2 · Monsoon destroys your paper stock

−৳20,000 to −৳60,000

Six to ten weeks at 80%+ humidity and your notebooks cockle, your washi tape loses tack and your printed boxes soften. The goods look fine on the shelf until you pack one and see it.

ResponseThe section 09 spec, set up before stock arrives: airtight bins, silica gel, off-floor racking, a ৳500 hygrometer logged weekly. And don't import paper — make it locally.

3 · Return-to-origin hits 25%

ROI 93% → ~60%

A quarter of COD orders refused at the door. You lose courier both ways plus the ad spend that won the order, and keep goods you've paid duty on.

ResponsePhone-confirm every order before dispatch. Free delivery for bKash prepayment. Blacklist repeat refusers. Set the RTO slider in section 14 to 25% and look at it properly before launching.

4 · Stock doesn't move

ROI halves at 2 turns

The quiet one, and the most likely. Every sale looks healthy while your capital sleeps for eight months. Nothing feels wrong until you can't afford to reorder.

ResponseGo narrow — 20 SKUs at rung 3, not 40. Hard rule: anything above 120 days of cover gets discounted, bundled or seeded. Prioritise corporate orders, which clear inventory in one transaction.

5 · Customs reassesses your declared value

landed cost +30–60%

NBR revises minimum import values regularly — 261 tariff lines this budget. If you priced against an optimistic landed cost, one reassessment turns your margin negative overnight.

ResponsePrice against lawful duty from day one. Don't compete at the bottom of the market — the up-market SKUs in section 04 have the headroom.

6 · The taka depreciates 10%

landed cost +10% per cycle

Costs in yen and RMB, prices in taka that lag. Not a risk — a trend.

ResponseReprice every cycle, not annually. Hold 10% of pricing headroom deliberately unused. Keep orders small and frequent early.

7 · Facebook restricts the page

revenue → near zero

Most of your Tier C competitors are a Facebook page with no fallback. Around 300,000 Bangladeshi businesses run this way.

ResponseOwn the domain and checkout from your first sale. Collect phone numbers from order one and build a WhatsApp list. Never let a platform hold your only customer relationship.

8 · Someone funded copies the position

margin compression

Nothing about importing Zebra pens is hard. Pixels adding a Japanese range, or a Daraz power-seller going legitimate, could take this in six months.

ResponseThe products aren't the moat — the content library, the authentication standard and your own packaging are. Start all three in month one, when they're free.

The stacking risk

None of these is individually fatal if you follow the ladder. What kills people is two at once — a slow-moving batch and 25% RTO, in the month you'd committed cash to a reorder. That's why section 14's ৳105,000 trough matters more than the ROI headline, and why rung 3 is deliberately ৳75,000 rather than ৳195,000.
